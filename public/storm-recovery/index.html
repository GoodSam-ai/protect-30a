Where the pilot stands

Contractor participation, funding, prices, and start dates are pending confirmation. Expressing interest does not book service, authorize property access, or create a payment obligation. Assistance and reimbursement are not guaranteed.

The proposed process

Clear steps. Traceable work.

01 / DOCUMENT

Understand the need

When secure intake opens, residents would provide debris photos, location, and access details. Assessments would distinguish estimates from contractor-verified quantities.

02 / COORDINATE

Plan the neighborhood

Nearby properties would be grouped into work blocks, with equipment and time estimates checked by the contractor.

03 / CONFIRM

Agree before work starts

Each job would need permission, a defined scope, price, responsible payer, and any required funding approval.

04 / COMPLETE

Follow through to the destination

Record completed cleanup, measured quantities, and hauling destinations. Suitable wood could become mulch where a receiving site approves its use.

Before you participate

Is cleanup available to book now?

No. This is the organizing stage. We will publish confirmed availability, service boundaries, prices, and instructions when the pilot is ready.

Will a grant or FEMA pay for my cleanup?

No funding or reimbursement is promised. Any assistance would depend on an identified program’s eligibility, covered work, and approvals. A community grouping or nonprofit intermediary alone does not establish eligibility.

What should I send today?

Use the existing Protect30A contact form for a planning inquiry. Include “Storm Recovery,” your neighborhood or HOA, your role, and a general description. Avoid sensitive personal information. Do not send precise photo coordinates or ownership documents at this stage.

What if the work is already covered elsewhere?

Check existing public services, insurance, or other assistance before paying or committing funds. The program would record coverage and prevent duplicate payment for the same work.

What about dangerous trees or power lines?

This page is not an emergency dispatch service. Stay clear of unstable trees and power lines; report electrical hazards to the utility and call 911 for an immediate emergency.
