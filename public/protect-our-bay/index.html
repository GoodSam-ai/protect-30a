OUR WATER. OUR NEIGHBORHOODS. OUR NEXT GENERATION.

The bay is part of our lives.

A morning on the water. A child’s first fish. A neighborhood we want to keep safe from flooding. These are the everyday reasons to care about what flows into Choctawhatchee Bay.

Whether you fish, paddle, work on the water or simply call this coast home, you deserve clear answers and a voice in decisions that affect it.

Make your letter personal.

“The bay matters to my family because ______. I live in ______, and I want our officials to ______.”

One honest local story helps officials understand why these four requests matter. Describe what you know firsthand; leave conclusions about contaminants to testing.

Add your story to the letter ↓

Four requests. One public deadline.

1. Open the records

Publish permits, outfall maps, inspections, monitoring and outstanding corrective actions.

2. Investigate the pathways

Use independent sampling, qualified laboratories and documented quality controls; include PFAS where warranted.

3. Establish the fish facts

Ask Health officials to assess fish-tissue evidence and the need for testing or consumption guidance.

4. Report within 30 days

Name responsible officials, identify gaps and propose a funded correction schedule.

Requested deadline: 30 days after the responsible body directs the review. No adoption or deadline start has been confirmed.

What is verified—and what remains unresolved?

Destin’s published stormwater program includes illicit-discharge detection, construction-runoff controls and long-term stormwater management for development. The four requests above are proposed community actions.

A PFAS-based fish-consumption advisory for Choctawhatchee Bay, project-specific contamination and insurer misconduct were not verified for this brief. This is not a finding that contamination is absent. Local fish-liver testing referenced on the Raven page must be assessed with its sampling and source limitations; it does not alone establish edible-tissue risk or a pollution source. Untreated runoff alone does not establish an unlawful discharge.

Legal tools and their limits · Florida Statutes §403.412

The statute provides a potential injunction route against environmental-law violations and failures to enforce. Ordinarily, a verified complaint to the responsible agency and a 30-day opportunity for action precede suit. It preserves a temporary-restraining-order route for immediate, irreparable harm. It bars actions under this section against operations covered by valid permits and complying with them. Specific law, standing, evidence, permit coverage and procedure require legal review; an ordinary advocacy email is not a substitute for statutory notice.
