Landscape stewardship

A planted place
for runoff to slow down.

A rain garden is a shallow planted area designed to receive runoff and let water soak into suitable soil. Plant selection and site conditions matter: Florida guidance recommends plants that tolerate both wet periods and dry spells.

Learn more from UF/IFAS Gardening Solutions and the EPA’s rain garden resource guide.

Start with suitability, not excavation

A rain garden is not appropriate for every site and is not a promise to resolve neighborhood flooding. Review soil drainage, groundwater, nearby structures, utilities, septic systems, access, and overflow with appropriate expertise before choosing a design. Arrange utility locating before digging.

The proposed process

From an idea
to a cared-for place.

01 / LISTEN

Understand the site

Describe the general location, existing runoff concerns, ownership, and community interest. Use the contact form for a planning inquiry; avoid sensitive documents or precise personal location data.

02 / ASSESS

Check what fits

Arrange a site review of drainage, soils, constraints, and the intended benefit. Confirm whether a rain garden or another approach is appropriate.

03 / PLAN

Agree on the project

Confirm design, plant choices, permissions, any required approvals, costs, responsible payer, and a maintenance owner before work begins.

04 / STEWARD

Record and maintain

For an approved project, document installation and follow-up observations. Keep a practical plan for plant establishment, maintenance, and checking how the site performs.

Questions before we grow

Is a microgrant available?

No grant funding is confirmed for this program. Any future assistance would have published eligibility, covered costs, application instructions, and approval requirements.

Can storm debris mulch be used here?

Only if material suitability and the site’s design requirements are verified and the receiving site approves it. Storm Recovery and Rain Gardens have separate scopes, budgets, and approvals.

Can I book an installation now?

No. We are collecting planning inquiries. Confirmed opportunities and participation instructions will be published when available.
