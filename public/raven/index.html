What the plans and environmental assessment cover

The packet includes a boundary/topographic survey, engineering construction plans, environmental assessment, traffic drawdown statement and existing-conditions materials.

Open the evidence and see the questions to test

Packet attachment index, page 1 · Environmental assessment begins on packet page 53.

The staff report describes an assessment of approximately 12.5 acres with no jurisdictional wetlands identified. Match its surveyed boundary to the proposed construction, drainage works and future-phase footprint. A current no-wetlands finding does not establish whether older conservation restrictions or mitigation obligations apply.

For drainage, obtain the native model, calculations, design storm assumptions, groundwater and receiving-water levels, outfall details, and maintenance obligations. Compare approved existing conditions with proposed conditions and impacts on neighboring properties. The public packet is not a substitute for independent verification of the model.

Wetland mitigation-bank credit transactions

DEP's published ledgers are another route to an impact-permit number. Cross-search project and predecessor names, owners and permit numbers; then trace any debit to the impact permit and approved map. No transaction has been verified as covering Raven in this review.

DEP mitigation-bank ledgers · Army Corps RIBITS bank records.

Check the ledger date, bank service area, credit type, quantity, debit approval and any return or correction. DEP handles mitigation-bank permitting within NWFWMD boundaries. A bank credit addresses its specified mitigation obligation; flood protection and pollutant transport require separate evidence.

Conservation easements, plats and original development approvals

Protect the water that connects us

Good Samaritan Institute testing reported PFAS in one pooled sample of 13 catfish livers collected at Ditch #10 in Santa Rosa Beach. This is an existing watershed concern—not a finding that Raven caused contamination. It does not measure edible fillets or establish a hydrologic connection to this project.

Our request: verify the drainage pathway, assess potential pollutant mobilization, and establish appropriate baseline testing before disturbance where supported by the evidence.

What the community is asking for

  • A complete corrected record with time for public review.
  • Independent drainage analysis covering volume, peak flow, water levels and flood duration.
  • Receiving-water protection supported by site-specific evidence.
  • Enforceable monitoring, maintenance and corrective-action duties.
  • Continuance while material questions remain; denial if applicable standards cannot be met.
